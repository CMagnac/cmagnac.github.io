I am a pharmacist, graduated from Paris University in 2007.

**September 2007.** I signed my first professional contract in French Guyana. I stayed there for a decade, learning how a community pharmacy really works.

Over the years, I started to see pharmacy management through the lens of the ancient Chinese game of **Go**. I am a Go player, and the more I played, the more I began to recognize some of its principles in pharmacy management.

In Go, the first objective is to build **territory**.

The same applies to a pharmacy. You need to build a solid foundation : be strong, move quickly, anticipate, and keep the initiative.

For me, **stock management is part of that foundation**. Before trying to optimize anything else, you need to understand what you have, what you need, what moves, what doesn't, and where your resources are being used. Stock is the territory you have to protect.

Then comes **influence**.

Influence gives you strength and creates opportunities. In a pharmacy, this means people: the team, patients, suppliers, colleagues, and all the relationships that make the organization work.

And this is much harder to understand.

Human resources cannot simply be reduced to numbers, rules or procedures. This is where management becomes empirical. You learn by observing, making mistakes, adapting and, above all, dealing with people.

But life is not a strategy game.

It is probably closer to **poker**.

You don't know all the cards. You cannot control everything. Sometimes you make the best possible decision and still lose.

And there is also a touch of **theatre**.

If life is a comedy, then we must play our roles seriously.

The only thing we can really do is stay focused on the present, keep learning, adapt to what happens, and try every day to become a better version of ourselves.

This is where pharmacy, strategy, technology, management and life meet for me.

**Welcome to my world.**
